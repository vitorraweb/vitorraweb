The measuring rule

A road is only compared with itself

In the before period alone, this truck's fuel use per kilometre differed by up to 38% between destinations. The road to Kitgum and the road to Paidha are simply not the same job.

So Masindi is judged against Masindi, Kitgum against Kitgum, and so on. Any other comparison would measure the road, not the device.

This is the same standard an independent reviewer, S-Line Motors, applied to this truck's records on 11 August. Their figures and ours agree.

Checked and settled with Hariss

Three questions came up. We closed all three.

  • ✓The Kamwenge dates were wrong in the export. The tracker file showed 1 April. Hariss confirmed the trip ran 1 to 4 August, and the record now carries the corrected dates.
  • ✓Two runs came home loaded. The Masindi and Yumbe runs after fitting carried Kinyara sugar on the return leg, while their before runs came back empty. Nearly double the work is not a fair comparison, so both are set aside, with the reason printed on the report.
  • ✓One load read slightly over rating. 30,160 kg against a rated 30,000. It is Hariss's own weighbridge figure, on a before trip, and within normal weighbridge variation. Accepted.

Every one of these decisions is recorded on the trial itself. Nothing is quietly dropped.

On fuel per kilometre this trial is flat. Three pairs of trips cannot yet show a 13.9% effect in either direction, and we will not put a number on evidence that does not carry one. The next slides show what the same records say from two other angles, and what would settle the question.

Before FET34.2 tonne-km / L
→

During the trial the truck twice carried freight home instead of running back empty. Fuel per kilometre punishes that. Cargo moved per litre rewards it, because it measures what a haulier actually buys diesel for.

To be plain: this rise measures how well the truck was used as much as how it ran, and cannot on its own prove the device. We show it because leaving it out would be just as misleading.

Against Hariss's own planning figure

Hariss budgets this truck at 2.2 kilometres per litre. It ran better than that budget in both periods, and the before period was the stronger of the two.

A planning figure is a target, not a measurement, so this lens frames the numbers rather than deciding them. The decision stays with the road-to-road comparison.

What settles it

Three things, two of them free

  1. This truck's trip history back to January, in the same export Hariss already sends. Their tracking system holds it, it costs nothing to pull, and it rebuilds a proper multi-trip baseline for every route. This is the single strongest move available.
  2. Confirm the Kamwenge distance. The run after fitting logged 701 km against 805 km before, to the same destination. One answer from the dispatch office moves or holds a seventeen-point swing.
  3. Keep driving pairs. Every further trip on a road that already has a before figure sharpens the answer. Three more would carry a verdict on their own.

We would rather show you a number that survives your questions than a bigger one that does not.
