If your Christmas stock is still on a ship or in a warehouse in Mombasa, the next three weeks matter. Three things are pushing transit times and costs up at once, and none of them is in an importer's control. What is in your control is when you book and how you plan.

## What is happening on the corridor

- **Mombasa is busy.** Importers are bringing in goods for Christmas and New Year sales, and the Nation reported in late October that more than 55 vessels were expected at Mombasa within two weeks, with the Kenya Ports Authority considering sending some traffic to Lamu ([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/counties/mombasa/ebola-festive-season-insecurity-pressure-mombasa-port-5639212)).
- **Truck drivers are being screened at the borders.** Because of the Ebola outbreak in the region, drivers crossing into Uganda go through mandatory health screening, and Kenya has prepared its busiest crossings, Busia, Malaba and Lwakhakha, for it ([WHO Africa, 9 September](https://who-africa.africa-newsroom.com/press/kenya-strengthens-ebola-readiness-at-its-busiest-land-border?lang=en)). Screening protects everyone, and it can also add time at the border.
- **Diesel costs more.** Diesel reached Shs 7,290 a litre at many Kampala pumps at the end of October ([Daily Monitor, 30 October](https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/diesel-hits-shs7-290-rising-fuel-costs-threaten-business-viability-5652850)). Road freight prices follow fuel.

## Five things to plan now

1. **Book this month, not next.** December is the busiest month on the corridor. The earlier a booking is confirmed, the more room there is when something takes longer than expected.
2. **Allow extra days at the border.** Plan for health screening at Malaba or Busia as a normal step, not a surprise, and set delivery dates your customers can trust.
3. **Get your paperwork right before the cargo moves.** Most delays that are in anyone's control are documents: invoices, packing lists and customs declarations that do not match. Check them before the container leaves the port.
4. **Ask for a quote that shows what is included.** With fuel moving, agree up front what happens to the price if diesel changes before delivery.
5. **Have one person who answers.** When a shipment crosses a port, a border and two customs systems, the questions come fast. One named contact who can see the whole journey saves hours of calls.
